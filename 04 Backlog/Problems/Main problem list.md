# Main problem list
> **Raw discovery notes** — the original pain-point research this product grew from.
> Preserved as-is: source material, never rewrite. V1 tackles the communication/progress
> cluster ([[ADR-002 V1 scope]]); the rest waits here.

### Workshop pain points:

1. Miss communication between departments
2. Progress on on-going jobs are unclear
3. manual recording on jobsheets
4. mechanic assessment
5. Talent pool. Hard to hire, hard to train, hard to hand over.

### Pain points per level:

**(L1)Service line (technicians, drivers, test crew)**

- Problem 1: Miss communication
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to see truck arrival, and types of jobs that are on going.
    
    I should be instantly alerted if a job is assigned to me.
    
    I should have clear notes on customer complaints, to further narrow my scope of diagnostics
    
    Reality:
    
    I am often un-aware of jobs that needed my attention.
    
    I am only alerted when customers informed front desk, which makes it critical from the start.
    
    I am usually wondering around without anything to do.
    
    I usually have to check everything due to customer complaints are not being passed fully.
    
- Problem 2: Technical knowledge growth
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should have a clear gauge on my technical abilities.
    
    I should have a clear goal to hit to further grow into.
    
    Reality:
    
    I have usually no clue where am I at technically.
    
    I always have no clue about my potential.
    

**(L2)Front Line (front desk)**

- Problem 1: Job progress
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should know the progress of each and every job
    
    I should know when which vehicle has been sent to where and when.
    
    I should be able to follow up job progress on time.
    
    I should be able to know critical jobs, so i can help/bring attention to it.
    
    I should know/ be able to estimate lead time for each job.
    
    I want to be able to answer to customers
    
    I want to be able to track jobs statuses.
    
    Reality:
    
    I lose track of jobs, especially I am not the one who handles it
    
    I don’t know how long has this job been out to a subcon
    
    I only know this job is critically slow when customer complains.
    
- Problem 2: Workforce at disposal
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to know who is available
    
    I should be able to know everyone’s job at hand
    
    I should be able to assign/optimize job assignment to the right mechanics
    
    Reality:
    
    I have no clue who is working on what
    
    I can’t evaluate the complexity of the job
    
    I have no clue where all mechanics are at each moment.
    
- Problem 3: Technician’s skill level (growth = current state - improvement)
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to know which technician is best for which job
    
    I should be able to train/ allow technicians to be paired on tasks, ensuring work quality and skill growth.
    
    Reality:
    
    I have no gauge on who is improving on which skill set
    
    I have no clue on the job’s complexity
    
    I am new, I don’t know any technicians.
    
    I gauge skill based on my impression, not data backed.
    
    I assign jobs based on technicians i know, not based on skills.
    

**(L2)Support Line (parts warehouse)**

- Problem 1: Ordering the right parts
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to refer back to pass history, based on model and order the right parts for it.
    
    I should have access to the history and feedback from customers in terms of success rate and usability of a part
    
    Reality:
    
    Every job is a new job even if its a frequent, new risk new knowledge.
    
    I take on un-needed extensive risk for ordering.
    
- Problem 2: Lose track of ordering
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should have a list of on going jobs, tracking its parts ordering progress
    
    Have a view on parts that are ordering, and at which supplier.
    
    I should be able to follow up consistently on parts.
    
    Reality:
    
    Forgot which supplier is handling which part
    
    Forgot which job is pending at which step
    
    Forget when was the last follow up.
    
- Problem 3: Confusing part numbers. (Permutations)
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to differentiate which part is this from, and for which model.
    
    I should be able to group them for price batching, making me aware of over charges from suppliers.
    
    Reality:
    
    Since it is so confusing, we do not care to compare at all
    
    I have no idea about the differences of quality, or service.
    
    I have no idea which supplier has the best skills for which kind of jobs.
    
- Problem 4: Parts pre-ordering
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be have be able to advise on pre-ordering parts in bulk to save costs and reduce lead time.
    
    I should have the data to back pre-ordering. Educated guess
    
    Reality:
    
    I buy parts singular.
    
    Customers will have to wait if there are no parts.
    
- Thoughts:
    
    1. Is parts differentiation important?
    2. What is its potential ? Is it worth going deep ?
    3. Ways to differentiate?
        1. Supplier, OEM parts origin (china etc), Brands, Warranty
    4. Price checking important? Since it will be charged to customer

**(L3)Mid-management**

- Problem 1: Workshop current health
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to track workshop health on a high level
    
    I should be able to notice stagnant jobs, sending help as needed.
    
    I should be able to notice jobs that are problematic before they come in.
    
    Reality:
    
    I have no clue of workshop’s situation, only by asserting and have a stroll behind the workshop.
    
    I can’t see jobs that are coming in, hence I am only able to know complex jobs when technicians are cooked.
    
- Problem 2: Talent pool management (we are as strong as our weakest link)
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be know which staff is good or bad.
    
    I should know which training should be provided to who
    
    Trainings sent should be on point to improve a certain staff
    
    I should have a set of expectations/benchmark when hiring.
    
    I should be able to make jobs modular, to make them easy to pass over.
    
    Reality:
    
    I barely send anyone to training, unless I know them personally
    
    Trainings are usually a hit or miss. We wend the wrong person to the wrong trainings.
    
    Jobs are too complicated in huge chunks. Handing over is simply too hard to do.
    
- Problem 3: Workshop Marketing
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to market the workshop with ease
    
    I should have contents to do so.
    
    I should be able to plan visits, and get customer feedback
    
    Reality:
    
    I am too caught up and unable to leave the workshop
    
    I do not have any content for display
    
    I do not have the skill set to create and publish the content.
    
    Customer feedback loop is hard to get and nail down.
    
- Problem 4: Customer Feedback loop
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to access customer feedbacks.
    
    Feedbacks from customers should be made with ease and precise, tailored specifically for each situation
    
    Reality:
    
    No feedback is enforced.
    
    No feedback is given in general.
    
- Problem 5: Technical knowledge pool
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should have a summary of a technical challenges at my disposal.
    
    I should have a built up of knowledge about the jobs around me.
    
    Reality:
    
    I usually have no one to refer to.
    
    Knowledge is “privatized”, and I do not know who has the knowledge to help me.
    

**(L4)Management**

- Problem 1: Cost / Profit
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to trace my cost to revenue ratio.
    
    I should be notified if any gets out of hand.
    
    Reality:
    
    I have no idea of anything till something is serious.
    
- Problem 2: Mid management performance
    
    Expectation:
    
    I should be able to gauge my mid management’s performance
    
    Reality:
    
    I have no clue what are they doing.
