# Voice and tone
Locked 2026-07-06. Derives from [[Positioning]] — if copy contradicts the spear, the copy is wrong.

## The voice: a good foreman
Calm, specific, economical. Says what's happening and what's needed. Never sells hard —
information does the persuading. If a sentence would sound odd coming from a competent
foreman on the shop floor, Knot doesn't say it.

## Register
**Neutral international English** ("vanilla"), locked 2026-07-06. One writer, one consistent
register, travels beyond Malaysia. Warmth comes from **specificity about workshop life**,
never from slang. *Deferred, not dropped:* local flavor (e.g. "towkay") may return in ad/social
variants once core copy is stable — test there, keep the core vanilla.

## Voice laws — verbal twins of the UI laws in [[Design system]]
1. **Copy whispers too.** No exclamation marks, no hype adjectives ("revolutionary", "seamless").
   Twin of "chrome whispers, status shouts."
2. **Read-once words.** A non-technical owner understands every sentence on first read. No
   SaaS-speak — "streamline", "empower", "workflow" are banned. Twin of "words over icons."
3. **Never sound expensive.** Enterprise polish loses the argument against a free whiteboard.
   Short sentences, honest claims, no jargon-as-authority.
4. **Specific beats slick.** Name real things: the board, the callback, the car waiting on a part.
   Concrete workshop imagery is the warmth budget.
5. **Show the promise, don't state values.** No "we believe" / mission-statement copy. Say what
   the owner sees: *every job, every handoff, on every phone.*

## Calibration examples
- ✗ "Streamline your workshop operations with real-time visibility!"
  ✓ "See every job in the shop — from the counter, the floor, or home."
- ✗ "Empower your customers with transparency."
  ✓ "Your customers watch the job themselves. The calls stop."
